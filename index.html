A study on language fairness in AI

Speaking বাংলা to AI can cost up to 5× more.

AI models read text in small pieces called tokens, and every token is billed. The same sentence in Bangla is cut into far more pieces than in English. That can mean paying more, waiting longer, and fitting less into the model's memory.

The problem in 3 steps

One word is enough to see it. Both words below mean the same thing.

  1. 1

    AI reads text in pieces

    Before a model can read anything, it cuts the text into tokens. A common English word is usually a single piece.

  2. 2

    Bangla is cut into more

    The same word in Bangla is broken into many more pieces. Some letters are even split into raw bytes.

  3. 3

    Every piece is billed

    AI companies charge per token. Same meaning, more pieces, higher price.

Why it matters

১ Documented

Higher cost

AI APIs bill per token. If a Bangla message needs 5× the tokens, a Bangladeshi startup pays 5× for the same feature.

২ Expected

Slower replies

Models write one token at a time. More tokens for the same answer means people writing in Bangla wait longer.

৩ Expected

Less memory

A model's context window is measured in tokens. Bangla fills it faster, so the model can read less of your document.

So why does it happen?

Tokenizers learn their vocabulary from training text that is mostly English, so common English words become a single token. Bangla is rare in that text, and every Bangla letter takes 3 bytes in UTF-8. The tokenizer often has no piece for it and falls back to raw bytes, splitting a single ক into two or three tokens. Newer tokenizers with larger, more multilingual vocabularies, like GPT-4o's, close much of the gap.

Questions

What is a token, in plain words?

A small piece of text. AI models never see whole sentences; they see a list of numbered pieces. In English a piece is often a whole word. In Bangla it is often a part of a letter.

Is my text sent to a server or to an AI company?

No. Counting happens inside your browser. The site only downloads each model's tokenizer file (its "dictionary") from Hugging Face. Your text stays on your device.

Are these the real tokenizers the companies use?

Yes, but some come from community copies on Hugging Face rather than the company itself; the "Tokenizers used" table marks which. GPT-4 and GPT-4o were checked against OpenAI's published encodings and all 16 counts matched. You can check any GPT number with the "Check it yourself" box.

Does more tokens mean the AI understands Bangla worse?

Not necessarily. This site measures cost in tokens, not understanding. Research has found that poor tokenization can hurt quality too, but that is a separate question this site doesn't test.

Why is BLOOM fair?

BLOOM's tokenizer was trained on data that deliberately included Bangla and other Indic languages, so it learned whole Bangla words as pieces. It suggests the gap comes from what a tokenizer learns from, not from the language itself.

Why only 8 sentences? Is that enough?

It is enough to show the effect clearly, but not enough for a final scientific answer. A larger study using FLORES-200, a standard benchmark of 1,000+ professionally translated sentences, is planned.

Does the ৳ figure show real prices?

No. It is a comparison: if a message costs ৳100 in English, how much would the same message cost in Bangla on the same model. Real prices differ by model and change over time.
